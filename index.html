Course overview

Foundations, methods and practices for developing software

Course›Overview

Foundations, methods and practices for systematically developing, evaluating and maintaining software. The course covers the lifecycle from requirements through design, implementation, testing and evolution, and then asks how AI changes each of those activities. AI is treated as an extension of software engineering, not as a replacement for it.

Illustrative course concept, prepared for a university teaching demonstration, October 2026. This is a worked proposal for how such a module could be structured. It is not an offering, and no enrolment, submission or examination system stands behind it.

Format and assessment

Course frame. The credit and contact-hour figures are a proposed module allocation, chosen to be proportionate to comparable modules.

LevelBachelor Computer Science, 3rd semester
Credits and format9 KP · lectures 4 SWS + exercises 2 SWS · 14 weeks
PrerequisitesOne programming language, and Git. The engineering fundamentals are taught from week 1, so the course is self-contained.
Practical componentA semester-long software project in teams of three or four
AssessmentPortfolio: semester group project 50 % · weekly exercises 30 % · final presentation and reflection 20 %

What you look at over the semester

By the end of the course you can:

  • select and apply an appropriate development process for a given situation;
  • specify, design, implement, test and maintain a software system as a team;
  • explain how AI agents participate in software engineering activities, and organise that work as a process with explicit checks;
  • assess whether an artifact has been adequately verified, and name what is missing, whoever or whatever produced it.

The last one is the spine of the course. It is asked first of your own work, and from week 9 of work an AI system produced.

How the semester is organised

Four blocks across 14 weeks: Software engineering foundations (weeks 1 to 4), Software quality and evolution (weeks 5 to 8), Systematically developing software with AI (weeks 9 to 12), and Professional practice and integration (weeks 13 and 14).

Weeks 9 to 12 are taught as one unit, Systematically Developing Software with AI, running from machine learning applications and generative AI through agents and agentic workflows to AI across the lifecycle.

Teams of three or four choose one of five systems in week 1 and build it across the whole semester, applying each method in the week it is taught. By week 9 there is a real system, with requirements, a design, tests and a pipeline, against which AI-assisted alternatives can be compared honestly. See the schedule, the weekly exercises and the semester project.

Using AI in this course

Use whatever tools you like. Document what you used. Never submit anything you cannot explain.

The policy applies from week 1, not from week 9. Transcripts are part of the deliverable: the final submission includes an AI-use log, and a statement of who approves what, on what evidence.

Further reading

Books

Software Engineering, Ian Sommerville, tenth edition, Pearson 2015. The standard reference for Blocks I and II: processes, requirements, architecture, testing, evolution.

Agentic Software Engineering: Building Trustworthy Software with Stochastic Teammates at Unprecedented Scale, Ahmed E. Hassan, 2026. The PDF is available from the book's own site. The closest thing there is to a textbook for Block III.

A Vibe Coder's Guide to Agentic Engineering, Adam Charnock, Dominik Grusemann and Traun Leyden. A practitioner's counterpoint to the two above.

Git and continuous integration

Pro Git, Chacon and Straub, second edition, free to read in full online. Chapters 1 to 3 and 6 are enough. Week 8 goes further with pipelines.

Python or Java basics

The Python Tutorial, Python Software Foundation, or Learn Java, the official Java tutorials. One language, well enough to read and review code you did not write.

Standards and bodies of knowledge

SWEBOK, Guide to the Software Engineering Body of Knowledge, IEEE Computer Society, free to download. For testing specifically, the ISTQB Certified Tester Foundation Level syllabus v4.0 is free and covers weeks 5 and 6.
