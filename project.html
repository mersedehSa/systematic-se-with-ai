Semester project

One system, built across the whole semester

Course›Project

Groups of three or four choose one of five systems in week 1 and build it across the whole semester. Each week's method is applied to that same system in that week's exercise, so the project is how the course is practised rather than something bolted on at the end. Deliverables and milestones are identical whichever system you pick.

Engineered first, then with AI

Weeks 1 to 8 build the system by established software engineering practice: requirements, architecture, implementation, review, testing, quality assurance, maintenance and a pipeline. From week 9 the same system is taken further with AI support. That ordering is deliberate. By the time an agent writes something for you, you have a specification to check it against, a test suite that knows what it does not cover, and your own conventional result to compare it with.

The menu

Home energy assistant

Ingests smart-meter and tariff data, schedules flexible loads such as charging before 07:00 or running the heat pump in the cheap window, and warns on anomalies.

Tariffs and scheduling give crisp acceptance criteria and hard limits.

Smart campus assistant

Study-room booking, events, and student requests routed to the office that can actually answer them.

Many small features and real stakeholders, so requirements work has something to bite on.

Disaster response coordination

Incident reporting, prioritisation and resource coordination for a volunteer organisation.

Conflicting requirements, several distinct workflows, and quality concerns that matter.

Sustainable mobility planner

Multimodal journeys across bus, bike and public transport, with a comparison of the options.

Realistic external APIs, and requirements that change while you are building.

Open-source project assistant

A tool that classifies issues, extracts requirements, finds the relevant code, proposes changes and reviews contributions.

The subject of Block III as the subject of the project. It still has to be engineered as software.

One point worth saying early: the project is a software system, not an agent. Even the project assistant option goes through requirements, design, implementation, testing and maintenance like the others.

Milestones

Four checkpoints and a final submission. Each one is the output of that week's exercise, not extra work.

MilestoneWeekWhat is due
M0Week 2Project chosen, toolchain running, requirements documented with acceptance criteria. The group is fixed.
M1Week 4Architecture designed with an ADR, first feature implemented and peer reviewed.
M2Week 8Tested and quality assured, one maintenance request handled, CI/CD running on the repository.
M3Week 12An increment developed with AI support, evaluated across the lifecycle activities it touched.
FinalWeek 13The system, the engineering evidence below, and the accountability statement.
PresentationsWeek 14Group presentation and individual reflection.

The final deliverable

A working system, and the documented engineering evidence that goes with it:

  • 1 Specification with acceptance criteria. What the system was asked to do, written so it can be checked.
  • 2 Test adequacy argument. Which criterion is covered by which evidence, and which criteria have none.
  • 3 Review and evaluation record. What was reviewed, by whom or by what, and what the evaluation found.
  • 4 Accountability statement. Who approves what, on what evidence.
  • 5 AI-use log, with the transcripts for the features it was used on.

Both halves are marked. The evidence record is not paperwork around the project: it is the part of the work this course is actually about.

Use whatever tools you like. Document what you used. Never submit anything you cannot explain.
