Semester projectOne system, built with agents, all semester

Groups of three or four choose one of five systems in week 1 and build it with agents throughout. Deliverables and milestones are identical whichever system you pick, because the exercises are written against "your project's next feature" rather than against a domain. One point is worth saying early: the project is a software system, not an agent. Even the project assistant option goes through requirements, design, implementation, testing and maintenance like the others.

The menu

Home energy assistant

Ingests smart-meter and tariff data, schedules flexible loads such as charging before 07:00 or running the heat pump in the cheap window, and warns on anomalies.

Tariffs and scheduling give crisp acceptance criteria and hard limits, and energy informatics is close to home here.

Smart campus assistant

Study-room booking, events, and student requests routed to the office that can actually answer them.

Many small features, real stakeholders, and an obvious case for specialized agents.

Disaster response coordination

Incident reporting, prioritisation and resource coordination for a volunteer organisation.

Conflicting requirements, several distinct workflows, and quality concerns that matter.

Sustainable mobility planner

Multimodal journeys around the city, across bus, bike and the Semesterticket, with a comparison of the options.

Realistic external APIs, and requirements that change while you are building.

Open-source project assistant

A tool that classifies issues, extracts requirements, finds the relevant code, proposes changes and reviews contributions.

AI for software engineering as the subject itself, and the strongest playground for the patterns. It still has to be engineered as software.

Milestones

Four checkpoints and a final submission. Each one is the output of that week's exercise, not extra work.
MilestoneWeekWhat is due
M0Week 2User stories with acceptance criteria, reviewed. The group and the project are fixed.
M1Week 4First feature merged, with the full agent transcript.
M2Week 7An agentic workflow for one of your own tasks, evaluated, with pass rate and variance.
M3Week 10Test adequacy argument: which criterion is covered by which evidence, and which has none.
FinalWeek 13The system and the full evidence package, below.
PresentationsWeek 14Group presentation and individual reflection.

The final deliverable

The running system, and with it:

  • 1 Specification with acceptance criteria. What the system was asked to do, written so it can be checked.
  • 2 Test adequacy argument. Which criterion is covered by which evidence, and which criteria have none.
  • 3 Review and evaluation record. What was reviewed, by whom or by what, and what the evaluation found.
  • 4 Accountability statement. Who approves what, on what evidence.
  • 5 AI-use log, with the transcripts for every merged feature.

The software is half of it. The evidence that it works is the other half, and it is marked as such.

Use whatever tools you like. Document what you used. Never submit anything you cannot explain.
