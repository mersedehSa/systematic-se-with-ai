ScheduleThe fourteen weeks

Four blocks. The first two build the vocabulary, the third walks the lifecycle phase by phase, and the fourth asks what the engineer is accountable for. Each lecture has an exercise, due before the following week, that feeds the semester project.

Lectures and exercises. Week 5 is the lecture given in the teaching demonstration.
Week Lecture Content Exercise
Block I · Foundations
1 Why software engineering has phases. The state of AI in development What the phases are for, and what the adoption claims and the evidence actually say Toolchain and first user stories
2 How LLMs work, for engineers Next-token prediction, tokens, context windows, training stages, strengths and limits One story, three models
3 Prompting and context engineering. The AI-assisted IDE Modes, indexing, configuration and memory files, and what they change in practice Your project's agent configuration
Block II · Agents
4 From LLMs to autonomous agents The agent loop, tools and tool calling, memory, skills, MCP One feature with a coding agent
5 Engineering Agentic Workflows: Patterns, Processes, and Quality Workflow or autonomous agent, five common patterns, evaluator and optimizer in depth, a first look at testing with AI Choose and apply an agentic workflow pattern
Block III · Agentic software engineering across the lifecycle
6 Multi-Agent Systems for Software Engineering Roles, topologies and coordination: who decides, who checks, who stops Specialized agents and explicit coordination
7 Reliability, Safety, and Evaluation of Agentic Systems Evaluating the agentic system itself: criteria, variance, and what an evaluation misses A ten-case evaluation
8 AI-Assisted Requirements Engineering Elicitation, specification and traceability when an agent does part of the asking Elicitation, twice
9 AI-Assisted Software Design and Implementation Design rationale, architecture decision records, and reviewing code nobody wrote by hand Two designs, one ADR
10 AI-Driven Software Testing and Quality Assurance Test design from requirements, agentic generation and repair, judging generated tests Criteria, tests, and what is missing
11 AI-Assisted Maintenance and Evolution Program repair, change in a system nobody fully remembers writing Verify someone else's patch
Block IV · Judgement and synthesis
12 Security of AI-generated code, and of agents What static analysis catches, what it does not, and fabricated dependencies Security triage
13 Accountability, intellectual property, the engineering role Approving software on a well-founded belief, and what that requires of you The accountability statement
14 Synthesis and project presentations Groups present their system and the evidence that it works Presentation and reflection
