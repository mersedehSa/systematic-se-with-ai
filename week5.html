Week 5 · Block II, AgentsEngineering Agentic Workflows: Patterns, Processes, and Quality

Software testing principles remain essential, but AI changes how software is produced, the verification challenges we face, and the testing activities we can automate. This week organises agentic work as a process: what the patterns are, how to choose one, and why the check inside a loop needs criteria somebody wrote down.

Learning objectives

By the end of this week you can:

  • recognise the common agentic workflow patterns and say how they differ;
  • select a suitable pattern for a software engineering task and apply it;
  • explain why evaluation inside a workflow needs explicit criteria, and what goes wrong without them.

Lecture outline

  1. One agent, and then several.
  2. Workflow or autonomous agent: what the choice actually commits you to.
  3. Five common patterns, and how to choose between them.
  4. Evaluator and optimizer in depth: the loop, a trace, four engineering decisions, and independence.
  5. Where agents sit in the lifecycle.
  6. A first look at testing with AI, including the in-class activity. The full lecture on testing is week 10.

Exercise 5 · Choose and apply an agentic workflow pattern

Three parts. Work in your project group.

Part A · Match the task to a pattern

Five software engineering tasks are given. For each one, say which pattern fits and why. More than one answer can be right, so the justification is what counts.

  • Analyse an issue and produce a structured report.
  • Classify incoming bug reports.
  • Analyse several independent modules.
  • Coordinate a change across files and components.
  • Generate, review and improve a solution.

Part B · Design a workflow for your own project

Pick one task from your project. Describe its characteristics. Choose one of the five patterns and justify the choice against the "use it when" criteria from the lecture. Then sketch the workflow, naming the roles of the LLMs, the agents, the tools and the people in it.

Part C · Build a small prototype

Implement the workflow, small, in whatever environment you prefer. Document what worked, what did not, and whether the pattern turned out to be the right one.

What to submit

  • 1 A workflow diagram.
  • 2 A short implementation or an execution trace.
  • 3 A half-page justification of the design decisions.

Due before week 6. Feeds milestone M2 in week 7.
